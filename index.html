About me

Engineer by training,
data scientist by practice.

I'm a Data Scientist currently based in Sydney, Australia, working at DataMesh Group where I build and deploy machine learning models and large-scale cloud data pipelines. My background is in Mechatronics Engineering — which means I approach data problems with an engineering mindset: rigorous, systematic, and focused on real-world impact.

I hold an MSc in Data Science and Innovation from UTS and an MEng in Artificial Intelligence and IoT from the University of Sonora. I've worked across three countries — Mexico, the USA, and Australia — and across industries from automotive manufacturing (Ford Motor Company) to fintech.

My work spans the full data science lifecycle: from raw data ingestion and pipeline engineering to predictive modelling, dashboards, and production deployment. I care about building things that are not just technically sound, but that actually move the needle for the business.

Outside of work, I'm passionate about the intersection of AI and physical systems — a thread that runs from my early IoT research to my current work in production ML.

01

Plant Disease Classification using Neural Networks

Developed a convolutional neural network to classify plant leaf diseases across multiple species. The model was trained on a dataset of over 20,000 images and achieved 89% classification accuracy — enabling faster, automated detection of crop diseases that would otherwise require expert manual inspection. The project covers the full ML pipeline: data preprocessing, augmentation, model architecture design, training, and evaluation.

02

Autonomic Hydroponic System with Real-Time Monitoring

Designed and implemented a self-managing hydroponic system prototype using IoT-connected embedded devices. The system integrates Autonomic Computing techniques to handle self-monitoring, self-regulation, self-healing, and auto-optimisation of resources — with all sensor data streamed and visualised in real time. Published and presented at the 9th International Conference on Control, Mechatronics and Automation (ICCMA).

03

Land Cover Classification via Satellite Image Segmentation

Applied unsupervised machine learning to identify areas of forage loss from satellite imagery. Using the K-Means clustering algorithm, distinct colour clusters were extracted from the images and used to create masks that differentiate biomass-rich areas from barren land — providing a scalable, low-cost method for environmental and agricultural monitoring across large geographic areas.
